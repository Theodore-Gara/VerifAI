VerifAI: Measuring AI Workplace Readiness Through Realistic Judgement Tasks

Meshaque Kalonji, Vusani Earl Mulaudzi, Theodore Nangara, Lwandle Naledi Chloe Mvelase, Mpho Mokoena

Faculty of Information Technology, Eduvos, Bedfordview, South Africa

Abstract

Employers are adopting AI faster than graduates can be assessed on using it well. Tool knowledge is easy to test and easy to forget; what decides whether a junior hire is trustworthy with AI is judgement: catching a hallucinated citation, knowing when not to automate a decision, and spotting a privacy or bias risk before it reaches a client. VerifAI is a Flutter application that measures AI workplace readiness through realistic, employer-style work. Learners find the flaw in an AI output, correct it, and justify the decision. Every graded attempt feeds a deterministic, server-side readiness score across four South African industry tracks, producing a 0 to 100 readiness profile, a tier, and an evidence trail a recruiter can inspect.

Introduction

No CV states whether a graduate can be trusted with AI, and a multiple-choice quiz measures recall rather than judgement. The gap is the product: an assessment that behaves like the work itself.

The app runs a daily loop: learn a concept, practise a flawed scenario, be assessed under exam-style conditions, see the evidence, and keep the habit going. Content covers Finance, Legal, IT and Software, and Business Consulting, with 144 authored scenario tasks and 576 answer options.

Assessment Engine

Verification. Either four-option choice, or a numbered AI output where the learner taps the flawed lines, types the correction, then the reason. Line-text scores 30 flaw, 40 fix, 30 reason, and needs 70 with a non-zero flaw and fix, so a plausible explanation cannot pass a task whose point is the correction.

AI Judgement. Decision (use AI, use with review, do not use) plus best-of-three reason. Decision scores 50, 25 or 0 for correct, neighbouring or opposite; reason scores 50 or 0. Pass 50.

Risk and Governance. Proceed or do not proceed, plus best-of-three reason, 50 and 0 each. Pass 50.

Interview Practice. An ungraded per-track question bank that feeds 10% of readiness as coverage.

Scoring is deterministic and server-side. The payload never contains the answer key, and no LLM grades a scored task, so the same answer always earns the same mark.

Readiness Model

Readiness is a weighted mean over the components a track actually offers. Each skill score is coverage: passed tasks divided by available tasks. A four-task skill therefore fills 25, 50, 75 then 100% as each task is passed, and never decays between sessions.

System Design

Client
Flutter app on Android, iOS and web: Riverpod state, go_router navigation, one API client.
Transport
HTTPS with a Firebase ID token; a 401 interceptor refreshes once, retries, then signs out.
API
Node.js 22 and Express on Render: 20 routers, 107 endpoints, rate limits, ETags, streaming.
Data
PostgreSQL on Supabase: 50 tables, 29 ledger-applied migrations, parameterised SQL.
Services
Firebase Auth, NVIDIA-hosted LLMs with a fallback model, and Tavily web search.

Figure 3: One API and one database behind three clients.

A submission is handled in one transaction: score the answer, write the attempt and its analytics row, recompute coverage and readiness if it moved, then award XP, tokens, streak and achievements. The app never computes a score.

Implementation

  • Scale: 34.5k lines (12.5k backend, 21.9k Flutter), 40 screens across 22 feature modules.
  • Navigation: five hubs, Home, Learn, Practice, Progress and Profile, with every other screen pushed from the hub that owns it.
  • Content: 5 lessons with 248 sections, 4 timed capstones over 100 questions, 16 industry cases, 9 rewards and 13 achievements.
  • AI: Verification Studio reviews pasted text, documents, links and citations; Vera and the in-task assistant stream over server-sent events and abort cleanly when the client disconnects.
  • Accessibility: light, dark and system themes, bundled fonts, OS text scaling, and semantic labels on custom-drawn scores.

The full loop is working end to end against the deployed API. A submission moves readiness, XP and tier immediately; feedback is stored and reopens from history or a deep link; and the same build ships as a web app and an installable APK. Continuous integration runs 186 backend and 55 Flutter tests, static analysis, a dependency audit and a secret scan on every push.

An internal audit closed 24 findings across three rounds, including fixed per-track readiness denominators, a shared rate-limit store in PostgreSQL, off-thread document parsing, and cached static payloads with ETag revalidation.

Conclusion and Future Work

VerifAI turns a vague hiring question, whether a graduate can be trusted with AI, into a measurable readiness score backed by evidence. The engineering that makes it credible is already in place: one authoritative scoring pipeline, a schema that evolves through reviewed migrations, streaming AI features that degrade gracefully, and server-side authorisation for every privileged action.

Outstanding work is scheduled rather than open: a testing phase to 30 October covering regression and a device matrix, Prompt Design content (scaffolded but not authored), a Workshop learner screen (API and moderation already complete), an embedded-Postgres integration suite, and row-level security hardening on the hosted database.
