01 · Introduction03 / 20

The problem

AI knowledge is easy to test.
Judgement is not.

Employers are adopting AI faster than graduates can be assessed on using it well. Tool knowledge is easy to quiz and easy to forget. What actually decides whether a junior hire is trustworthy with AI is the judgement around it.

Catch what is wrong

A hallucinated citation. A fabricated figure. Code that silently swallows an exception. The output looks confident and is wrong.

Know when not to automate

Some decisions carry legal, financial or human consequences. The correct answer is sometimes “use AI with a human in the loop” - or not at all.

Spot the risk first

Privacy, bias, POPIA and client confidentiality. By the time a risk reaches a client, the damage is already done.

No CV states it. No multiple-choice quiz measures it. That gap is the product.

01 · Introduction05 / 20

The core loop

One action, then a number that moves

Home never opens on a wall of charts. It opens on the one thing worth doing next. Submitting a task updates readiness, XP, streak and achievements in a single transaction - the server is the source of truth, and the app only renders what it is told.

01
Home recommends the next task
Unseen first, in a difficulty band suited to level; least-recently attempted rotates back in as “Practise again”.
02
The learner answers
Pick the flawed lines and write the fix, choose the correct option, or make a decision plus a justification.
03
The server scores and commits
Attempt, analytics row, coverage, readiness, XP, tokens, streak and achievements - one transaction.
04
Feedback explains, then offers the next step
What was right, what was missed, and a full-screen next task.
05
Home reflects the new reality
Readiness, tier, XP and a fresh recommendation.

One request, not five

Home is GET /api/dashboard: profile, progress, dimension scores and one recommended task. It replaced five calls, three of which ran an expensive selection query and were then discarded.

Practice is honest

Repeat attempts are labelled Practise again and earn no new coverage - a practice pass pays half XP, once. You cannot farm readiness by re-doing the same task.

Server-side “today”

Streaks are anchored to Africa/Johannesburg, not the phone’s clock or the server’s UTC - a check-in just after midnight cannot land on yesterday.

03 · The assessment engine10 / 20

Fairness rules

Scoring that cannot be gamed

Verification line-text: the fix is the skill

At a flat 60, tapping the flawed lines (30) plus a plausible reason (30) passed without fixing anything. Line-text therefore requires ≥ 70 and a non-zero flaw and fix score. The reason alone can never carry the task.

XP is earned, never printed

XP is score-proportional with a difficulty ceiling - easy 50, medium 70, hard 100. A wrong answer earns 0 XP. Practice pays half, once.

Options never leak their score

The task payload omits every option’s score and the correct flag. The answer key exists only in the database until the attempt is scored.

One transaction, six effects

A submission commits atomically:

  • the attempt and its analytics log row
  • feature coverage (passed ÷ available)
  • readiness and tier, only if coverage moved
  • XP, level and token grant
  • streak advance
  • new achievements, token grants batched

Idempotent by construction

The token ledger has a unique (user, reason, ref) key, so a retried request cannot double-credit. Practice passes are counted once.

03 · The assessment engine11 / 20

Closing the loop

Feedback that survives the session

A score without an explanation teaches nothing. Every attempt is stored with a title snapshot, the selected lines, the rationale and the pass flag - so feedback is a real record, not a route-local variable.

Three honest states

Correct, incorrect and error are distinct screens with different copy and actions. Network failure shows a retry, never a fabricated score.

Re-fetchable

GET /api/history/attempts/:id is owner-scoped; /feedback/:attemptId renders the stored result after a restart or deep link.

Searchable history

Paginated with limit/offset, free-text search and type filters, plus infinite scroll. Rows open their feedback.

What the learner sees

  • Score and pass/fail against the dimension threshold
  • The correct lines and a model correction
  • Why their reason did or did not land
  • XP earned, streak, and any achievement unlocked
  • One clear next action: retry, or continue

What the team sees

  • Answer logs per task and per option
  • Per-track progress and funnel in the admin portal
  • CSV exports for users, attempts and track progress
  • Moderator queue for extended and rubric answers

04 · Readiness & growth12 / 20

The readiness model

One score, six weighted components

Readiness is a 0–100 weighted mean. The denominator is the sum of the weights of the components the learner’s track actually offers - so an empty module cannot silently cap a learner, but a module left untouched still counts as zero coverage.

Verification0.30
AI Judgement0.30
Risk & Governance0.20
Learning (capstones)0.20
Prompt Design0.10
Interview0.10

Each skill score is coverage: passed tasks ÷ tasks available. A skill with four tasks fills 25 → 50 → 75 → 100% as each is passed. It climbs with evidence; it does not decay between sessions.

Inputs, not opinions

Skill scores come from stored coverage; Interview from questions practised ÷ questions offered; Learning from the average best capstone result. Nothing is self-reported.

Tiers follow the score

Novice0 – 49
Developing50 – 69
Proficient70 – 84
Advanced85 – 100

Why it is hard to fake

Readiness is written on the server on every scored attempt, from stored coverage. Three strong answers cannot reach Advanced if two modules are untouched - coverage counts.

04 · Readiness & growth13 / 20

The habit loop

XP for effort, tokens for progress

Readiness measures competence; the reward economy keeps people coming back. XP is never spent. Tokens are - granted by levels and achievements, written to an append-only ledger.

XP & levels

Score-proportional per difficulty, 0 for wrong. Levels step by +60 XP and unlock spendable tokens.

Streak

Daily, anchored to Africa/Johannesburg. Server dates mean a timezone cannot break it.

Achievements

13 definitions, granted in one batched statement on the submission that earns them.

Rewards

9 catalogue items with original artwork, atomic redemption, and a ledger that cannot double-credit.

Leaderboard & milestones

Global and per-track boards with the learner’s own rank always live; the board itself is cached per (track, limit) for 60 seconds. A covering index on (xp DESC, readiness_score DESC, user_id) keeps it off a full scan.

Activity, not a number

A shared activity view projects task attempts, lab practice and capstones into one timeline, and the 30-day chart groups on stored dates instead of converting a timezone per row.

The learning ecosystem

More than a quiz app

Scenarios test judgement. The rest of the product builds the knowledge and habit around them - and every part feeds the same profile.

AI Lab

5 lessons, 248 sections, 240 activities. Track-specific and global content, per-section progress, a source library with licence metadata, recall practice.

Capstones

4 assessments, 100 questions. Timed, attempt-capped, pass mark and reward. Objective questions auto-mark; extended answers queue for a moderator, with AI suggesting rubric marks.

Verification Studio

Daily mood check-in, private journal with undo, self-care activities, breathing exercise, weekly summary. Private to the learner.

An app-wide AI coach and an in-task assistant. Both stream over SSE, keep inspectable memory, and never hand over the answer.

05 · Engineering & delivery15 / 20

Architecture

One API, one database, one source of truth

Client
Flutter (Android, iOS, web) - Riverpod state, go_router navigation, Dio HTTP. Screens read a provider; providers call one ApiClient. That single seam is what makes 55 widget and flow tests possible without a network.
Transport
HTTPS with a Firebase ID token on every protected call. A 401 interceptor refreshes once, retries, then signs out cleanly.
API
Node.js 22 + Express on Render - 20 routers, 107 endpoints, rate limits, in-process caches, ETags, SSE streaming, a bounded document-parsing worker pool.
Data
PostgreSQL on Supabase - 50 tables, 29 ledger-applied migrations, a shared pg pool, parameterised SQL throughout.
Services
Firebase Auth for credentials and token verification; NVIDIA-hosted LLMs for analysis, chat and marking with a fallback model; Tavily for live web search.
Delivery
Render blueprint (migrate, then start) · Firebase Hosting for the web build · GitHub Actions for tests and the APK artefact.

05 · Engineering & delivery16 / 20

Engineering decisions

What we fixed after auditing ourselves

Three review rounds, 24 findings closed. The ones that matter most are below - each replaced a working-but-wrong design, not a missing feature.

Security

Every protected route verifies a Firebase token; roles come from admin_roles in the database, so a moderator cannot promote themselves. Bodies are size-capped per route, SQL is parameterised, the answer key never ships early, submitted verification material is never stored, and CI runs gitleaks.

Correctness

The readiness denominator is fixed per track, so starting a second module no longer lowers the score. Streaks use one configured timezone. Practice passes count half, once. Readiness is skipped when nothing moved.

Testability

One ApiClient seam, a mock client for widget tests, and a 200% text-scale regression test so accessibility cannot silently break layout.

Performance

Home is one composed request instead of five; analytics, leaderboard and static catalogues cache for 60 s with strong ETag/304; indexes follow the actual queries; the timezone conversion is gone from chart queries.

Reliability

Rate limits live in PostgreSQL, so two API instances share one per-account budget. Document parsing runs off the request thread with byte, page, queue and timeout caps. Streaming aborts upstream work when the client disconnects and sends keep-alives during model silence.

Accessibility

Light, dark and system themes; bundled Inter/Manrope (no runtime font download, no reflow on first paint); OS text scaling; semantic labels for scores, streaks and cards.

Authoring still goes through the admin portal and seed files - there is no dedicated CMS review workflow.

The Supabase tables are reachable through the API only in practice, but row-level security is not enabled on the public schema. Enabling RLS (the API connects as owner) is a low-risk hardening step before submission.

Where we are

A working product, not a prototype

VerifAI turns a vague hiring question - “can this graduate be trusted with AI?” - into something measurable, and backs it with the engineering that makes it credible.

  • The learner works through realistic, flawed AI output and is scored on the judgement behind the answer.
  • The result accumulates into a readiness score, a tier and a body of evidence an employer can inspect.
  • The habit is supported by XP, tokens, streaks, rewards, a wellness centre and an AI coach.
  • The engineering is a single authoritative scoring pipeline, a schema that evolves through reviewed migrations, and graceful degradation for every AI feature.
  • The delivery is real: deployed API, deployed web build, installable APK, tested on every push.
  • The next phase is testing and hardening - not new features.

Appendix AA1

Delivery checklist

Things easy to forget tomorrow

Warm the API before you start

The API runs on a free tier that sleeps when idle. Open the web app 5 minutes before you present so the first request is not a 30–60 s cold start in front of the panel.

Demo with a lived-in account

Use a learner who already has attempts, so Progress, history and readiness look alive. A brand-new account makes the product look empty.

Two fallbacks, not one

Install the APK on a phone and keep a 60–90 s screen recording of the full loop. If the network dies, play the clip and keep talking.

Have the links ready

Web: verif-ai-5a435.web.app · API health: /api/health. Put a QR code on the last slide or a handout.

Confirm your slot length

This deck assumes ~16 minutes plus questions. If you have 10, cut slides 7 and 13 first - keep the demo and the readiness model.

One driver, four talkers

Agree who holds the laptop. Handover cues: “…and Theodore will show how that is scored.” Rehearse the switch at least twice.

Say the same numbers

Use the figures in this deck consistently: 144 tasks, 107 endpoints, 241 tests, 50 tables. Mixed numbers invite doubt.

Be first to name the gaps

Prompt Design content and the Workshop screen are unfinished. Say so before the panel finds it - it reads as control, not failure.

Appendix BA2

Q&A preparation

The questions you will be asked

QuestionShort answer
Why not let an LLM grade the answers?Determinism, fairness and cost. A scored task must give the same result twice and be auditable. LLMs are used where judgement is genuinely open - rubric suggestions for extended answers - with a moderator making the final call.
How do you stop people gaming readiness?Coverage denominator fixed per track, distinct passed tasks only, server-side scoring, practice passes count once at half value, and the answer key never leaves the database before submission.
What about POPIA and privacy?Submitted verification material is never persisted; API keys stay server-side; Vera receives only bounded context with explicit privacy guidance; wellness content is private to the learner.
Does it scale / what does AI cost?60-second caches, ETag/304 on static content, per-account rate limits shared across instances, a fallback model, and deterministic scoring on the critical path so AI is never in the hot loop.
What is still missing?Prompt Design content, Workshop mobile UI, a real-Postgres integration suite, offline mode, and the testing phase (regression, device matrix) due 30 October.
Is it actually deployed?Yes: API on Render, database on Supabase, web build on Firebase Hosting, and a release APK built by CI. Every flow demoed runs against the live API.
